# How can I show I had my thesis, design, writing or code first?

*PROOF · updated 2026-10-18*

> **Short answer.** Before you share it, prove the exact version you have, and keep that proof. Fingerprint the file (or the whole folder) and have the fingerprint time stamped by an independent witness: that shows this exact work existed, in your hands, by that moment. It is useful when someone later presents the same idea as theirs, because your proof predates their claim. Be clear about the limit: a time stamp shows you had it by a date, not that you created it, and not who owns it. In many countries copyright exists from the moment a work is made, without registering anything; a proof of when you had it supports that, alongside drafts, emails and version history. For anything valuable, talk to a lawyer; this is not legal advice.

## Why before matters

The moment you send a design to a client, a manuscript to a publisher or code to a repository, copies exist that you do not control. A proof made before that moment shows your version came first, whatever happens to the copies.

## Build a trail, not a single proof

Prove each important version as you go: the first sketch, the draft, the version you send. A series of proofs, each later than the last, tells the story of the work better than any one document. Keep the drafts themselves, too.

## A simple habit

1. **Prove before you share.** Make a proof of the exact files you are about to send.
2. **Prove each milestone.** First version, major revisions, the final. Folders can be proved as one.
3. **Keep originals and proofs together.** And keep your drafts, emails and version history, which support the same story.
