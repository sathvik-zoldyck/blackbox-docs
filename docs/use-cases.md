# Most of these are *ordinary Tuesdays.*

Rashnova is two things at once. Every day it is a record of your own machine, which mostly tells you that nothing happened. Occasionally the device leaves your hands, and then the same record becomes evidence. Below: four of the first kind, eight of the second.

## Accountability

A tamper evident record changes behaviour before it changes an outcome. Most weeks it simply confirms that nothing happened, which is its own kind of useful.

## Visibility

Windows keeps no readable account of which programs touched which files. This one is yours, it stays on your machine, and only you can open it.

## Accountability at work

A record you can check, which can help you show what happened on a computer. Rashnova is not certified under any law or standard, and using it does not make an organisation compliant.

## The weeks where *nothing happens.*

### Your own machine, on an ordinary week

Nothing happened this week. Almost certainly. You worked, you browsed, you closed the lid at night. The Readout confirms it in a line, and tells you the two or three things that were, technically, new: an app that read a folder it had not touched before, a drive you had not plugged in since March. Mark something as you, and that same thing stops being raised. Twenty seconds, whenever you look.

› readout week 06 · verdict = nothing unusual

› chain verified · 1,284 entries · sealed

### The computer everyone at home uses

One machine, four people, one set of documents nobody has ever discussed. Not a surveillance problem, a housekeeping one. Who installed the thing that now runs at startup? When did the external drive last get plugged in, and by whom? The record answers it without anyone having to accuse anyone. Tell the people who use the machine that it is running; that is the baseline everywhere, and on a family computer it is also just decent.

› process_start setup.exe · account = guest

› usb_inserted vid=0951 pid=1666

### The app you installed and are not sure about

A free utility, a converter, a game launcher. It works. It also asked for more than it needed, and now you would quite like to know what it actually touches. Windows will not tell you. The Readout will: which folders it read, how often, and whether it started doing so the day you installed it or three weeks later. Not a verdict on the app, a record of its behaviour, which you can judge yourself.

› file_read ×41 · \Documents · SupportTool.exe

› first seen 14 days after install

### The machine that started at 02:14

You woke up and the fans were running. Almost every time, it is Windows Update doing exactly what it is scheduled to do, and the boring answer is the correct one. But you had no way to check, so you either shrugged or you worried. The record shows the boot, the time, and whether anyone signed in afterwards. Usually nobody did. Knowing that is worth more than assuming it.

› awake 6m · scheduled maintenance window

## And the hours when *someone else has it.*

### A laptop or phone in for repair

Unsupervised access to an unlocked machine, for hours, by someone you will not meet again.

Arm Repair Mode at the counter. Come back to a sealed report: who signed in, what was opened, what plugged in.

### Fleet servicing and hardware upgrades

Employee laptops go out for SSD swaps and come back with no account of what the vendor touched.

A per device session record for every machine, and an audit trail your policy can point at.

### Vendor visits to clinical workstations

A scanner needs maintenance; the attached workstation holds patient records.

Evidence that no patient data was opened during the service window, or evidence that it was.

### Preserving evidence for a dispute

A device was accessed without authorisation and the only account of it is a memory.

A hash chained record with a verifiable manifest, and a documented chain of custody.

### Maintenance on payment systems

A POS or payment server needs updating, and cardholder data sits on the same machine.

A record of the maintenance window you can check, to show what happened.

### Third party access to a build machine

Hardware support on a server holding proprietary source code and signing keys.

Every file copied to a USB drive is on the record, including the bulk copies standard monitoring misses.

### A student's laptop, three years of research on it

A screen replacement should not cost you your thesis or your logged in accounts.

A record of the repair window, free.

### A freelancer holding client confidences

Contracts and client files on the machine you just handed to a service centre.

Proof, if a client ever asks, that their material was not opened while it was out of your hands.

Where a device is shared, issued to staff, or used by anyone other than you, disclose that it is recording. That is the safer baseline everywhere and in many places it is required. Rashnova is built for the owner of a machine, never for monitoring a person.

## Install it once. *Find out on Sunday.*

Free for individuals, with a free account to download. Switch on recording once, and the first Readout arrives at the end of the week.
