# Use cases

*Most of these are ordinary Tuesdays.*

Rashnova is two things at once. Every day it is a record of your own machine, which mostly tells you
that nothing happened. Occasionally the device leaves your hands, and then the same record becomes
evidence. Below: four of the first kind, nine of the second.

- **Accountability.** A tamper evident record changes behaviour before it changes an outcome. Most
  weeks it simply confirms that nothing happened, which is its own kind of useful.
- **Visibility.** Windows keeps no readable account of which programs touched which files. This one
  is yours, it stays on your machine, and only you can open it.
- **Compliance.** Verifiable activity records that support DPDP Act, GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA and PCI DSS
  evidence requirements.

## The weeks where nothing happens

### Your own machine, once a week

Nothing happened this week. Almost certainly. You worked, you browsed, you closed the lid at night.
The Readout confirms it in a line, and tells you the two or three things that were, technically,
new: an app that read a folder it had not touched before, a drive you had not plugged in since
March. You mark them as you, and they stop being raised. Twenty seconds, once a week.

### The computer everyone at home uses

One machine, four people, one set of documents nobody has ever discussed. Not a surveillance
problem, a housekeeping one. Who installed the thing that now runs at startup? When did the external
drive last get plugged in? The record answers it without anyone having to accuse anyone. Tell the
people who use the machine that it is running; that is the baseline everywhere, and on a family
computer it is also just decent.

### The app you installed and are not sure about

A free utility, a converter, a game launcher. It works. It also asked for more than it needed, and
now you would quite like to know what it actually touches. Windows will not tell you. The Readout
will: which folders it read, how often, and whether it started doing so the day you installed it or
three weeks later. Not a verdict on the app, a record of its behaviour, which you can judge yourself.

### The machine that started at 02:14

You woke up and the fans were running. Almost every time, it is Windows Update doing exactly what it
is scheduled to do, and the boring answer is the correct one. But you had no way to check, so you
either shrugged or you worried. The record shows the boot, the time, and whether anyone signed in
afterwards. Usually nobody did. Knowing that is worth more than assuming it.

## And the hours when someone else has it

| Situation | The risk | What the record gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A laptop or phone in for repair** | Unsupervised access to an unlocked machine, for hours, by someone you will not meet again. | Arm Repair Mode at the counter. Come back to a sealed report: who signed in, what was opened, what was plugged in. |
| **Lending it to family or a friend** | Someone you trust uses the computer for a while, and nobody remembers afterwards what happened. | Handover Mode records the same as Repair Mode, and only your PIN ends it. |
| **Fleet servicing and hardware upgrades** | Employee laptops go out for SSD swaps and come back with no account of what the vendor touched. | A per device session record for every machine, and an audit trail your policy can point at. |
| **Vendor visits to clinical workstations** | A scanner needs maintenance; the attached workstation holds patient records. | Evidence that no patient data was opened during the service window, or evidence that it was. |
| **Preserving evidence for a dispute** | A device was accessed without authorisation and the only account of it is a memory. | A sealed record with a documented chain of custody. |
| **Maintenance on payment systems** | A point of sale or payment server needs updating, and cardholder data sits on the same machine. | A record for the maintenance window that supports PCI DSS evidence requirements. |
| **Third party access to a build machine** | Hardware support on a server holding proprietary source code and signing keys. | Every file copied to a USB drive is on the record, including the bulk copies standard monitoring misses. |
| **A student's laptop, three years of research on it** | A screen replacement should not cost you your thesis or your logged in accounts. | A record of the repair window, free. |
| **A freelancer holding client confidences** | Contracts and client files on the machine you just handed to a service centre. | Proof, if a client ever asks, that their material was not opened while it was out of your hands. |

Where a device is shared, issued to staff, or used by anyone other than you, disclose that it is
recording. That is the safer baseline everywhere, and in many places it is required. Rashnova is
built for the owner of a machine, never for monitoring a person.
