# Every device deserves a witness. *We build it.*

Alcyone Secure is a cybersecurity company from India. We build **Rashnova**, the evidence layer for Windows: a sealed record of what happened on your computer, and proof that a file existed, exactly as it is. Our thesis has not changed: security is not just prevention. Security is accountability.

Anyone who has handed a laptop to a repair shop knows the feeling. The machine that holds your photos, your money, your work: in a stranger’s hands, behind a counter you can’t see past. And every security product you own has gone quiet, because none of them were built for this.

Antivirus looks for malware. It was never built to notice a person. The technician browsing your files, the colleague at your unlocked desk, the intern with day one access to everything: they don’t break in. They are handed the keys, and they look exactly like normal work.

Rashnova is our answer. Not prevention, a witness. A recorder that writes down what happens to your device in a form nobody can quietly rewrite, encrypted so that nobody, including us, can read it but you.

We are building it deliberately honest: local first, sealed before it leaves the device, provable when it matters. If we do this right, you will never have to wonder what happened while your device was out of your hands.

Trust is good. Proof is better.

## The paid version came second. *On purpose.*

Q. You spent years on this. Version one earns nothing. You could have built the paid company product first, why didn't you?

A. Because Rashnova was never a startup idea. A friend and I were tracking online channels that traded stolen private photos, most of them of women, most of them Indian, none of them aware the pictures existed. We reported channels; new ones appeared the next day. One night I asked an operator where the photos came from. His third source broke me: repair shops. Technicians copying customer drives before repairs, paid per dump. The customers never knew. I couldn't stop thinking about it. So I built the product that didn't exist.

Q. What did version one cost you?

A. A small room, one PC, more research papers than I can count, more nights than I want to remember. I lost people along the way. I almost stopped, more than once. I didn't. The money making version stayed on the shelf the whole time. This one had to exist first.

Q. Then where does the money come from?

A. Companies. The recorder is free forever: that's not a launch offer, it's the point. The cloud layer we're building gets funded the way GitHub is: businesses pay for fleet features, and their money holds the price for individuals at zero, or close enough not to matter. Students, women, parents: the people who need this most shouldn't have to pay to protect themselves from an industry that profits off their trust.
